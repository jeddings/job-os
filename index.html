The Job Search Operating System

Jeff Eddings

In January 2026, after spending six years at Meta, I was laid off. That started a five-month job search, and along the way, I ended up building a system to run the search itself.

My search started like many others: searching job boards. Like most people, I started with saved searches: “staff technical program manager,” “platform product manager,” and a handful of similar terms. What I quickly realized was that job boards had some inherent issues, starting with keywords returning roles that pattern-matched the words and brought back less relevant roles. Also, the best companies often don't post on job boards at all, and a significant portion of what did show up were sponsored, meaning relevant roles I was looking for were mixed in with whoever paid to be listed. And I kept seeing the same roles over and over in these searches. So I did what a PM and TPM do: I defined the problem and built a program to solve it.

The core of the system was a Google Sheet anchored by a Companies tab. Instead of waiting for job boards to surface opportunities, I built a priority queue of companies likely to have relevant roles and searched their careers pages on a prioritized rotating schedule. I was frequently able to apply within the first day or two of a posting going live, which matters at the staff level because hiring managers review the first wave of applications before their ATS queue becomes overwhelming. The Companies tab became the starting point for everything else: role evaluations with structured scoring, a canonical resume built for fast customization, and a story library for interview prep. The result was faster signal, better coverage, and a system that got smarter the longer I ran it.

I wrote this as a record of what I built. What follows is the full system, including the tracker, the scoring framework, the resume approach, and how I used Claude throughout. If you're in a search, take what's useful.
